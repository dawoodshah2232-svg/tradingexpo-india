Position Sizing in Trading: The Math That Matters More Than Strategy

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Two traders can take identical trades with identical win rates — and one thrives while the other blows up. The difference is position sizing: how much capital each trade risks. It is the least glamorous and most decisive skill in trading.

Master this maths and every strategy you ever learn gets safer. Ignore it and no strategy can save you.

The 1% rule and fixed-fractional sizing

The professional standard: risk no more than 1% of your account on any single trade (2% is the aggressive ceiling). On a ₹5,00,000 account, that is ₹5,000 maximum loss per trade — so a trade with a ₹10 stop-loss per share gets 500 shares, while one with a ₹50 stop gets 100.

Position size is therefore derived from the stop distance, never chosen first. The formula is simple: shares = risk amount ÷ (entry − stop). This single habit enforces discipline automatically.

Why sizing beats prediction

Consider a strategy with 50% win rate at 2:1 reward-to-risk. Risking 1% per trade, a ten-trade losing streak — statistically inevitable over a career — costs under 10% of the account. Risking 10% per trade, the same normal streak destroys 65% of capital and ends most traders psychologically.

Drawdowns are a mathematical certainty; ruin is a choice. Sizing is how you choose survival.

The Kelly criterion: powerful but dangerous

The Kelly formula computes the theoretically optimal bet size from win rate and payoff ratio — but it assumes perfectly known probabilities and produces terrifying drawdowns in practice. Professional gamblers and traders who use Kelly typically apply 'fractional Kelly' (a quarter to a half of the full value).

For most traders, fixed-fractional 1% is simpler, more robust and easier to follow under stress. Complexity you abandon mid-drawdown is worse than simplicity you maintain.

Position sizing for options buyers and leveraged trades

Options buyers face a sizing subtlety: the premium is the maximum loss, which tempts traders to buy 'cheap' far out-of-the-money options in large quantities. But a ₹5 option that expires worthless 80% of the time needs very different sizing than equity with a stop-loss — the win rate is low and the payoff distribution is skewed. Size options positions by premium risked as a fraction of capital (0.5–1% for speculative long options), and never let the low ticket price justify oversized quantity. Ten thousand rupees in lottery-ticket options is not 'small' — it is ten thousand rupees of near-certain decay.

For leveraged futures and intraday positions, compute size from the stop distance in points times lot quantity, exactly as with equities — leverage does not change the formula, it only means smaller capital controls the position. And add a portfolio-level rule leveraged traders often miss: cap total margin utilised at a fraction (say 50–60%) of available capital, keeping dry powder for drawdowns and preventing the forced exits that margin calls impose at the worst moments. The traders who survive leveraged products are not the ones who predict best; they are the ones whose sizing survives being wrong repeatedly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 1% rule in trading?

Risk no more than 1% of your trading capital on a single trade. It ensures that even long losing streaks cause survivable drawdowns rather than account-ending ones.

How do I calculate position size?

Position size = (account × risk%) ÷ (entry price − stop-loss price). The stop distance determines the size — never decide share quantity before knowing your stop.

Should I increase size after winning streaks?

Fixed-fractional sizing increases size automatically as the account grows. Avoid discretionary size-jumps after wins — overconfidence position-sizing is a classic way to give back streaks.
