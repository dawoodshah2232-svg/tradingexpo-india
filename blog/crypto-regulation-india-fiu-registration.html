Crypto Regulation in India: FIU Registration and What It Means for You

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

In March 2023, India brought virtual digital asset service providers under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, requiring exchanges serving Indian users to register with the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND). It was the single biggest regulatory milestone for Indian crypto.

Here is what FIU registration means in practice and how to use it as a safety filter when choosing where to trade.

What FIU-IND registration requires

Registered entities must implement KYC verification, monitor transactions for suspicious activity, file prescribed reports with FIU-IND, and maintain detailed records. In short, a registered exchange operates much like a traditional financial institution on the compliance front.

The government has shown it will act against non-compliant platforms — including restricting access to offshore exchanges that served Indian users without registering. Registration is not optional decoration; it is the price of legal operation in India.

How to check your exchange is registered

FIU-IND publishes information about registered reporting entities. Check whether your platform publicly states its FIU-IND registration and verify the claim rather than taking a badge on a homepage at face value. Be especially careful with platforms that changed domains or operate through mirror sites.

If an exchange avoids the question of Indian compliance entirely, treat that silence as your answer.

What registration does and doesn't protect

FIU registration means the platform follows anti-money-laundering rules and verifies identities — it does not insure your deposits, guarantee solvency, or protect you from hacks. The collapses of supposedly solid global platforms taught the industry that compliance and custody safety are different things.

Protect yourself further: enable every security feature (2FA, withdrawal allowlists, anti-phishing codes), keep only trading capital on exchanges, and move long-term holdings to self-custody wallets where you control the keys.

KYC and data privacy: what exchanges collect and why

FIU-IND registration obliges exchanges to run full KYC: government ID verification, address proof, PAN linkage and increasingly video or liveness checks. They must also monitor transactions — flagging structuring (splitting transfers to dodge thresholds), rapid in-and-out movements and other suspicious patterns — and file reports with the authorities. This is the same compliance machinery banks operate, and it means your complete trading history on a registered exchange is an open book to regulators. Trade accordingly: every transaction should be one you can explain.

That visibility cuts both ways for privacy. Minimise your data footprint sensibly: use strong unique credentials so a breach elsewhere does not cascade, be cautious about which personal documents you share with smaller platforms, regularly review the permissions you have granted to exchange apps, and understand each exchange's data retention and sharing policies before completing KYC. The uncomfortable truth is that regulated crypto trading and financial privacy are largely incompatible — a trade-off every user accepts when choosing compliant platforms over unregulated ones. Choose with eyes open, secure what you share, and keep records that match what the exchange reports about you.

Frequently asked questions

What is FIU-IND registration for crypto exchanges?

Since March 2023, virtual digital asset service providers serving India must register with the Financial Intelligence Unit as reporting entities under the PMLA, implementing KYC, transaction monitoring and regulatory reporting.

Does FIU registration make an exchange safe?

It means the exchange follows AML/KYC rules, but it does not insure deposits or guarantee solvency. Combine the registration check with security practices like 2FA and self-custody for long-term holdings.

Can I use offshore exchanges from India?

Offshore platforms serving Indian users are expected to register with FIU-IND; authorities have restricted access to non-compliant ones. Using a registered, compliant platform is the safer path.
