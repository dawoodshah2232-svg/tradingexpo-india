Demat Account in India: How to Open One and Choose the Right Broker (2026)

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Every Indian market journey starts with two accounts: a trading account (to place orders) and a demat account (to hold securities electronically). Opening them in 2026 takes minutes on a phone — but choosing well repays attention.

The complete guide: process, documents, costs and broker choice.

Documents and the eKYC process

You need PAN (mandatory), Aadhaar for e-sign, a bank account in your name, and a cancelled cheque or bank statement for verification. The flow: fill the online form, complete Aadhaar OTP eKYC, upload income proof for derivatives segments, e-sign, and do a quick video verification (IPV) — SEBI requires it.

Most accounts activate within 24–48 hours. If a 'broker' skips KYC or IPV, you are not dealing with a legitimate broker.

CDSL vs NSDL and the charges

Your demat account sits with one of two depositories — CDSL or NSDL — via your broker (the depository participant). For practical purposes they are equivalent; broker choice matters far more than depository choice.

Understand the charges: account opening (often free now), annual maintenance (AMC), and per-transaction debit charges when you sell. Compare the full schedule, not just the headline 'free account' offer.

Choosing your broker

Verify SEBI registration first (our 5-minute verification guide), then compare: brokerage plans vs your trading frequency, platform quality on your devices, customer support responsiveness, and fund withdrawal ease. Open small, test everything including a withdrawal, then commit.

For a full framework, read our broker selection guide — the decision deserves an hour of your time.

Nomination, e-DIS and account safety essentials

Opening the account is step one; securing it is the ongoing job. First, add a nominee to your demat account — SEBI has pushed hard on nomination because unclaimed securities of deceased holders without nominees create painful legal processes for families. It takes minutes online and prevents enormous future distress. Second, understand e-DIS (electronic delivery instruction slip): the modern replacement for paper DIS booklets, used when transferring securities out of your demat account. Enable it properly and guard the authorisation mechanism, because e-DIS is the channel through which holdings leave your account.

Third, lock down account access: unique strong passwords, 2FA everywhere offered, and transaction alerts on both email and SMS so any unexpected debit surfaces immediately. Fourth, verify your holdings independently — CDSL and NSDL both offer direct holding statements (CAS) that bypass your broker, letting you confirm your securities exist as reported. Check these periodically; the habit takes minutes. Finally, keep your contact details and bank mandates current, and be instantly suspicious of any communication asking you to transfer securities or share login credentials — brokers and depositories never ask. Your demat account holds your wealth; defend it with the seriousness that deserves. Finally, review your nominee details, email, mobile number and bank mandate once a year — outdated information causes exactly the delays that nomination and e-DIS were designed to prevent. A focused fifteen-minute annual review keeps your market passport valid, reachable and fully under your control.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need for a demat account?

PAN (mandatory), Aadhaar for eKYC/e-sign, bank proof (cancelled cheque or statement), and income proof if you want derivatives segments. The process is fully digital including video verification.

CDSL vs NSDL — which is better?

They are India's two equivalent depositories; your broker connects to one. Broker quality, charges and platform matter far more than which depository holds the account.

How long does demat account opening take?

Typically 24–48 hours for a clean digital application. Delays usually come from name mismatches between PAN, Aadhaar and bank records — keep them consistent.
