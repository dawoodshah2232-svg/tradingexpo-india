Stablecoins Explained: What Indian Traders Should Know in 2026

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Stablecoins — tokens designed to hold a steady value against the dollar — became crypto's plumbing: the parking spot between trades, the remittance shortcut, the DeFi collateral. Then several high-profile failures reminded everyone that 'stable' is a design goal, not a guarantee.

Here is what Indian traders should understand before trusting significant capital to a stablecoin.

How stablecoins actually work

Fiat-backed stablecoins (like USDT and USDC) claim backing by reserves of cash and short-term securities, redeemable for dollars. Algorithmic stablecoins instead tried to hold their peg with code and incentives — a design that failed catastrophically in 2022, wiping out tens of billions.

The lesson: all stablecoins are not equal. A token's peg is only as strong as its reserves, its redemption process and its issuer's transparency — all of which deserve scrutiny before you treat the token as 'dollars'.

The real risks: de-pegs, freezes and regulation

Even major stablecoins have briefly traded below their peg during market stress. Issuers can also freeze addresses on their blocklists — a feature, not a bug, from a compliance perspective, but a reminder that you hold an IOU, not cash. And in India, stablecoin holdings are VDAs subject to the same 30% tax and 1% TDS regime as other crypto.

Counterparty risk concentrates silently: millions treat stablecoins as risk-free cash equivalents until the week they discover otherwise. Size your exposure as what it is — unsecured exposure to a private issuer.

Sensible uses for Indian traders

Used with eyes open, stablecoins are genuinely useful: moving value between exchanges quickly, parking profits during volatility without converting to INR (and triggering TDS each way), and accessing dollar-denominated DeFi yields. Keep allocations modest, prefer transparently audited issuers, and diversify across more than one token.

For core capital, boring wins: INR in regulated accounts for trading, hardware-wallet crypto for long-term conviction. Stablecoins are a tool for transit, not a vault.

Yield on stablecoins: DeFi returns versus the real risks

Stablecoins promise something seductive: dollar-denominated yields of 5–15% through DeFi lending and liquidity provision, far above bank rates. The returns are real when they arrive — but they are payment for risks that marketing understates. Smart-contract risk means the code holding your funds can be exploited; the history of DeFi is punctuated by nine-figure hacks. Protocol risk means the lending platform itself can fail or be drained. And the yield itself is often paid in volatile governance tokens or depends on leverage loops that unwind violently in downturns. A 'stable' 12% yield built on three layers of leverage is not a savings account.

If you participate, apply position discipline: allocate only what you can lose, prefer battle-tested protocols with multiple audits and long track records, understand exactly where the yield comes from (if you cannot explain it, you are the yield), and watch for the warning signs — spiking utilisation rates, falling token incentives, or yields far above market norms. For Indian users, add the tax layer: DeFi transactions are VDA transfers with the same 30% and TDS implications, and tracking them is genuinely hard. Most traders are better served keeping stablecoins as a transit tool and earning yield, if at all, in small experimental allocations.

Frequently asked questions

Are stablecoins safe?

Fiat-backed stablecoins from transparent issuers are relatively stable but carry issuer, reserve and regulatory risk — they are not equivalent to holding dollars or insured deposits. Algorithmic stablecoins have a history of catastrophic failure.

Are stablecoins taxed in India?

Yes. Stablecoins are virtual digital assets under Indian law, so gains are taxed at 30% and transfers attract 1% TDS under the VDA framework.

USDT vs USDC: which is safer?

Both are large fiat-backed stablecoins; compare their reserve attestations, redemption processes and regulatory posture rather than relying on brand familiarity. Diversifying across issuers reduces single-point risk.
