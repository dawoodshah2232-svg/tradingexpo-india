How to Start Trading in India in 2026: A Step-by-Step Beginner's Roadmap

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Starting trading in India in 2026 is simultaneously easier and harder than ever: easier because accounts open in minutes on your phone, harder because the noise — tips, gurus, guaranteed-return schemes — is deafening. What beginners need is not more information but the right sequence.

Here is that sequence, step by step.

Step 1–2: Account and broker

Open a demat and trading account with a SEBI-registered broker — the process is fully digital via Aadhaar-based eKYC. Choose using our broker selection guide: verify registration, compare charges, test the platform. This administrative step is also your first fraud filter — legitimate brokers, proper KYC, no shortcuts.

Fund the account with money you can afford to lose. Trading capital is risk capital by definition; rent money in a trading account is a disaster with a login.

Step 3–4: Learn the foundations

Spend 4–8 weeks on structured basics: market mechanics, order types, candlestick reading (our price action guide), and risk management (the complete guide). One quality source beats ten conflicting gurus — finish one curriculum before sampling others.

Then paper-trade for at least a month: execute your planned strategy in a simulator or spreadsheet with real prices. Most beginners discover here that their 'strategy' was actually a collection of impulses.

Step 5–6: Go live small, then scale on evidence

Start live with the smallest size that still triggers real emotions — emotions are part of what you are training. Trade one setup, risk 1% per trade, journal everything (see our journal guide). Scale position size only after 2–3 months of consistent, journaled, plan-following profitability.

Expect the learning tax: most traders pay tuition to the market in year one. Keeping that tuition small through sizing is what lets you survive to year two, when skill starts compounding.

The first 90 days: a week-by-week starter plan

Turn the roadmap into a calendar. Weeks 1–2: open your demat and trading account, verify your broker's SEBI registration, and complete one structured basics course — market mechanics, order types, candlestick fundamentals. Weeks 3–4: study risk management exclusively (position sizing, the 1% rule, stop-loss placement) and set up your trading journal before your first practice trade. Weeks 5–8: paper-trade one simple strategy — a single setup, defined entry, stop and target — logging every trade with screenshots and R-multiples. Aim for 30+ paper trades; the goal is process familiarity, not paper profits.

Weeks 9–12: go live with minimum size that still triggers real emotions, risking 1% per trade on the same single setup. Expect performance to dip as psychology enters — this is normal and educational. End each week with a journal review: am I following the plan? Which deviations cost money? At day 90, conduct a full review: win rate, average R, deviation rate, and an honest assessment of whether you are ready to continue, adjust, or extend the learning phase. Ninety disciplined days build more real skill than a year of casual dabbling — and they reveal, with evidence, whether trading suits you before significant capital is at risk.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start trading in India?

You can open accounts with a few thousand rupees, but practical learning capital is typically ₹25,000–₹50,000 minimum so position sizing works. See our capital guide for the detailed breakdown.

How long does it take to learn trading?

Expect 6–12 months of serious study and practice before consistency, and 2–3 years before genuine competence. Anyone promising faster is selling something.

Should I quit my job to trade?

No — not until you have 12+ months of living expenses saved plus a full year of profitable live trading covering those expenses. Trade part-time (swing trading fits well) until the numbers force the decision.
