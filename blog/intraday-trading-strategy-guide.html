Intraday Trading Strategy Guide: What Actually Works (and What Doesn't)

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Intraday trading has the best marketing and the worst statistics in retail finance. Studies of day-trader profitability across markets consistently find that a large majority lose money — yet intraday remains India's most popular trading style. Both facts can be true: the style is brutally hard, and a disciplined minority does make it work.

This guide is about what that minority does differently.

The costs nobody calculates

Before strategy, do the maths most intraday traders skip: brokerage, STT, GST, stamp duty and exchange charges on every round trip, plus the bid-ask spread. On small, frequent trades these costs can exceed 1–2% of traded value daily — a hurdle your strategy must clear before it earns a rupee.

Track your actual cost per trade for a month. Many struggling traders discover they have a cost problem disguised as a strategy problem.

Setups with real edge: opening range and pullbacks

Two intraday structures have survived decades of scrutiny. The opening-range breakout trades the first 15–30 minute range once price commits with volume — capturing the day's directional impulse. The pullback entry waits for the morning trend to pause and enters on resumption, offering better risk-reward than chasing.

Both demand the same discipline: predefined levels before the bell, no mid-trade improvisation, and immediate exits when the thesis breaks. The setup is 20% of intraday success; execution discipline is the other 80%.

Risk rules that keep you alive

Risk a fixed small fraction of capital per trade (1% is the professional standard), cap daily loss at 2–3% after which you stop trading for the day, and never average down a losing intraday position. Revenge trading after a loss is the number-one account killer — our trading psychology guide dissects it.

These rules feel restrictive until the day they save your account. Every veteran intraday trader has a story about the rule that kept them in the game.

The first 15 minutes: a professional's opening routine

Amateur intraday traders spend the first 15 minutes chasing; professionals spend it observing. The opening routine: watch how price behaves relative to the pre-market range and prior day's high/low, note the initial volume character (conviction or hesitation), and identify the opening range whose breakout or breakdown will define the early trend. No trades in the first 5–15 minutes is itself a strategy — it filters the chaotic opening auction where spreads widen, algos battle, and retail traders donate. Your first planned trade should come from levels identified before the bell, not from excitement at the screen.

Prepare the routine the evening before: mark key levels on 3–5 instruments maximum (focus beats coverage intraday), note scheduled data releases for the day, and define your scenarios — 'if Nifty opens above X with volume, I watch for pullback entries toward Y; if it gaps down through support, I stand aside for 30 minutes.' Write the daily loss limit on a sticky note beside the screen. This 20-minute evening preparation and 15-minute morning observation convert intraday from reaction to execution — and that conversion is the entire difference between the traders who survive the statistics and those who become them.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of intraday traders make money?

Academic and regulatory studies across markets consistently show a large majority of frequent day traders lose money net of costs. Treat intraday as a professional skill requiring capital, training and discipline — not a shortcut to income.

What is a good risk-reward for intraday?

Aim for setups offering at least 1.5:1 to 2:1 reward-to-risk. With a 40–45% win rate at 2:1, the maths works; at 1:1 it usually does not, once costs are included.

Should beginners start with intraday trading?

Honestly, no. Start with longer timeframes (swing or positional) where decisions are slower and costs lower. Move to intraday only after proving consistency on slower charts with real money.
