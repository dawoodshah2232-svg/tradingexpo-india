What to Look for When Comparing Brokers at a Trading Expo

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 25, 2026·6 min read

← Back to all articles

A trading expo floor is the fastest way to compare brokers in India: a dozen booths in one hall, live platform demos, and real humans answering your questions instead of chatbots. It can also be overwhelming — every stall claims the lowest brokerage and the best platform, and the loudest booth is rarely the best broker.

The difference between a useful expo visit and an expensive mistake is arriving with a comparison framework instead of relying on impressions. This guide gives you one: what to check first, which questions actually reveal quality, and the red flags that should send you walking to the next booth.

Step one: verify regulation before anything else

Before you compare a single fee, confirm the broker is registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). Ask for the SEBI registration number at the booth, then verify it independently on SEBI's own records — never rely on a banner, a badge, or a confident-sounding representative.

Why so strict? Because regulation is the foundation every other comparison stands on. An unregistered operator's attractive pricing is worthless: your money has no investor-protection framework behind it. This is also the step most visitors skip, which is exactly why it belongs first on your list. Our detailed walkthrough, how to verify a broker's SEBI registration, shows the full process step by step.

Compare the full cost, not the headline brokerage

"Zero brokerage" on a banner tells you almost nothing. The true cost of a broker is the sum of many small charges, and booths are designed to show you only the flattering ones. Ask for the complete charges schedule in writing and compare these lines across brokers:

  • Brokerage model: flat fee per order versus a percentage of turnover — which is cheaper depends entirely on your trade size and frequency, so run your own typical trades through both.
  • Account charges: account opening fees and the annual maintenance charge (AMC) for your demat account.
  • Transaction extras: depository (DP) charges on sell transactions, exchange transaction charges, and statutory levies that apply regardless of broker.
  • Call-and-trade and support fees: some "discount" brokers charge meaningfully for phone-based orders or assisted trades.
  • Fund transfer and withdrawal costs: how you move money in and out, and what each movement costs.

A broker who hands you the full schedule without hesitation is telling you something good about their culture. One who waves it away with "don't worry, it's all very low" is telling you something else.

Put the platform through its paces

The expo's unique advantage is the live demo — use it aggressively. Ask the representative to log into the actual trading platform (not a marketing video) and walk through your real workflow: placing a limit order, modifying it, setting a stop-loss, checking your positions and margins.

While they demo, ask the questions that matter in live markets:

  • How did the platform perform during the last major volatile session — were there login or order-rejection issues?
  • What order types are supported (bracket, cover, GTT, after-market)?
  • Is there a full-featured mobile app, and does it support the same order types as the desktop?
  • What charting and screening tools are included at no extra cost?
  • Is there a demo or paper-trading mode so you can practise before committing funds?

Watch how the representative reacts to hard questions. Confident, specific answers suggest a product team that has actually solved these problems; vague reassurances suggest the opposite.

Test the support you will actually get

Everyone promises "great support" on a banner. At the booth, test it like a mystery shopper: describe a realistic problem — a rejected payout, a margin shortfall notice, a trade dispute — and ask exactly how you would resolve it, through which channel, and how long it takes.

Note the practical details: support hours versus market hours, languages offered, whether phone support exists or everything is ticket-based, and how complaints escalate if the first response doesn't solve your issue. A broker's support quality is invisible until the day you desperately need it — the expo is your one chance to inspect it before that day arrives.

Match the product range to how you actually trade

A broker can be excellent and still wrong for you if it doesn't cover your markets. Confirm coverage for everything you trade or plan to trade: equity delivery and intraday, equity derivatives, commodities, currency derivatives, mutual funds, IPOs, and bonds or government securities if relevant.

Also clarify the service model. Discount brokers compete on price and platform; full-service brokers add research reports, advisory, and relationship managers at higher cost. Neither is universally better — the right choice depends on whether you value independent research or prefer to do your own analysis and pay less for execution.

Your booth checklist: the exact questions to ask

Print this list or save it on your phone, and run through it at every shortlisted booth. The brokers worth your business will answer all of them directly.

  • What is your SEBI registration number, and where can I verify it?
  • Can I have the complete charges schedule in writing — including DP charges, AMC, and call-and-trade fees?
  • Walk me through placing and modifying a stop-loss order on your live platform.
  • What happened on your platform during the last highly volatile trading day?
  • Which segments and order types do you support, and is there a demo account?
  • How do I reach support during market hours, and how do complaints escalate?
  • What are the fund withdrawal timelines and any associated charges?
  • What exactly are the conditions and expiry of today's expo offer?

Red flags: when to walk to the next booth

Some signals should end the conversation immediately. Walk away from any broker that promises guaranteed returns or "risk-free" profits — no legitimate broker does this, and in India it violates basic regulatory norms. Walk away from pressure to deposit money on the spot, from representatives who can't produce a SEBI registration number, and from anyone who asks for your existing broker's login credentials or an OTP — legitimate onboarding never needs those.

Also be wary of the subtler flags: fee answers that stay vague after two direct questions, demo platforms that look nothing like the product described, and "today only" offers presented with no written terms. The expo floor rewards the visitor who is willing to say "I'll think about it" and keep walking.

Frequently asked questions

How many brokers should I compare at an expo?

Three to five is the sweet spot. Fewer and you have no real basis for comparison; more and the booths blur together while decision fatigue sets in. Shortlist in advance if the exhibitor list is published, and give each shortlisted broker at least 15–20 minutes including a live platform demo.

Can I trust the offers brokers advertise at expos?

Treat expo offers as starting points, not facts, until you have the full terms in writing. Ask for the complete charges schedule, the offer's conditions and expiry, and confirm the broker's SEBI registration independently. Genuine promotions survive scrutiny; pressure tactics do not.

Should I open an account on the spot or decide later?

Decide later, with almost no exceptions. Use the expo to gather information, watch live demos and collect written fee schedules. Real offers do not evaporate on Monday morning, and a broker worth your money will still welcome you after the event.

What is the single most important thing to check at a broker booth?

SEBI registration. Before costs, platforms or offers, confirm the broker is registered with SEBI and check the registration on SEBI's own records. An unregistered operator's attractive pricing is worthless — regulation is the foundation every other comparison stands on.
