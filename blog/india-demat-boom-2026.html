India's Demat Boom: 237 Million Accounts and the Making of a Trading Nation

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 25, 2026·6 min read

← Back to all articles

In August 2026, Indian households opened demat accounts at the fastest pace in seven months. Nearly 3.3 million new accounts were added — the highest monthly addition since January — pushing the country's total past 237.7 million, according to 360 ONE Capital Research. After a quiet spell from early 2025 through mid-2026, the retail investor machine is humming again.

The headline number is easy to celebrate and easy to misunderstand. It is both a milestone and a question mark: millions of accounts, but far fewer active traders. To understand where Indian retail trading really stands in late 2026 — and what it means for you — you have to look past the headline.

Two depositories, one retail wave

India's demat system runs on two depositories, and the August data shows how decisively the retail wave has chosen its favourite. CDSL reported 191 million accounts, up 1.5% month-on-month, with its incremental run-rate jumping 17.2% sequentially to 2.74 million — taking its market share to roughly 83.9%. NSDL grew to 46.7 million accounts, a respectable 1.1% monthly rise, but added only about 0.53 million new accounts.

The split reflects how each depository is positioned. CDSL has become the home of the discount-broker generation — the millions who opened their first accounts on phones, in minutes, through app-first brokers. NSDL, older and anchored in the institutional and large-investor segment, remains healthy but is simply not where the retail onboarding frenzy lives. The number printed on your demat account tells you which one you are with: a CDSL account number is a 16-digit numeric ID, while an NSDL account number starts with "IN" followed by 14 digits.

The 19.3% reality check

Here is the number that should sober every reader: only about 46 million of those 237.7 million accounts showed up as active clients on the NSE in August. That is an active-to-total ratio of 19.3% — and the trend is drifting the wrong way, down from July, even as accounts surge. Year-on-year, active clients were actually 0.5% lower.

Some of the gap is arithmetic. One investor can hold multiple demat accounts — the real count of unique investors is estimated at around 135 million, well below the account total. Some accounts exist purely for IPO applications, opened in a flurry and never funded again. Others belong to people who tried trading once, took a loss, and walked away.

The lesson is plain: a demat account is a door, not a trade. India's brokerages know this — it is why client activation, education and engagement now matter more than raw onboarding numbers. For you, the takeaway is simpler: if you opened an account and stopped there, you are part of the 80%, not the 19.3%.

What brought the crowd back: the IPO machine

August 2026 was the busiest month of the year for India's primary market, and that is what pulled people back. Twenty-three IPOs raised ₹22,448 crore in a single month, with two more offer-for-sale transactions raising ₹31,660 crore — and giants including Jio and the NSE itself expected to hit the market in the coming months. As Sriram Velayudhan of IIFL Capital Services put it, buoyancy in the IPO market has been a key driver of the jump in new demat accounts.

The rally was not in the benchmarks. The Sensex fell 1.5% and the Nifty 1.2% in August — yet the BSE MidCap 150 gained 1.6%, the SmallCap 250 rose 2.4% and the Microcap 250 jumped 6.2%. In other words, the segments where retail investors actually hunt for listing-day pops stayed hot while the headline indices cooled. Strong small- and midcap performance raises return expectations from IPO debuts, and that expectation is what opens accounts.

It is a familiar cycle: IPOs bring in first-timers, listing gains bring in their neighbours. The question is what happens next — whether those accounts evolve into investors, or expire quietly once the IPO calendar thins.

From accounts to active traders

For the industry, the conversion gap is now the central battleground. Discount brokers are winning the onboarding race — Groww has widened its lead among active users, with roughly 16.9 million, while Zerodha serves around 17.5 million clients, per Business Today. But the firms that turn a curious IPO applicant into a disciplined market participant will be the ones that endure: education content, paper-trading tools, risk guardrails, and communities where beginners can learn without being sold hype.

This is where events and expos earn their keep. A demo on a broker's expo stand, a masterclass on risk management, a panel on how professionals size positions — these are the moments that convert an account into a trader. Hands-on exposure does what app notifications cannot.

If you just opened an account, start here

Three honest rules for the newest members of the 237.7 million. First, treat your first year as tuition — invest in learning before leverage, as our beginner's guide lays out. Second, remember the arithmetic of derivatives: a SEBI study found that 1.10 crore F&O traders lost a combined ₹1.81 lakh crore over three years, with only 7% turning a profit — the futures and options market is a tax on the unprepared. Third, build the boring infrastructure early: a position-sizing plan, a trading journal, and the risk-management discipline our complete risk guide covers.

India's retail trading boom is real — the numbers prove it. Whether it produces a generation of skilled investors or just a generation of accounts depends on what each new holder does after the welcome email. Frequently asked questions

How many demat accounts are there in India in 2026?

Total demat accounts stood at 237.7 million at the end of August 2026, according to 360 ONE Capital Research. Because one person can hold multiple accounts, the number of unique investors is estimated at around 135 million.

Why is CDSL growing faster than NSDL?

CDSL has become the depository of India's retail wave — it holds about 83.9% of demat accounts, serves the discount-broker ecosystem and added 2.74 million accounts in August 2026. NSDL remains strong among institutional and large investors.

Is every demat account holder an active trader?

No. NSE's active client base was about 46 million in August 2026 against 237.7 million total demat accounts — an active-to-total ratio of 19.3%. Many accounts are dormant, duplicates, or used only for IPO applications.
