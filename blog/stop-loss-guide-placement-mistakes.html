Stop-Loss Guide: Where to Place It and 5 Mistakes That Guarantee Losses

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

The stop-loss is the most important order in trading — it is the price at which your thesis is proven wrong and you exit with a small, planned loss instead of a large, unplanned one. Yet most traders place stops arbitrarily: at round numbers, at fixed percentages, or wherever feels comfortable.

Here is how to place stops that actually protect you.

Structure first: stops below invalidation

A stop belongs where your trade idea is proven wrong — below support for a long, above resistance for a short, beyond the swing point whose break negates the setup. This 'invalidation point' approach means every stopped trade taught you something: the market structure genuinely failed.

Percentage-based stops (always 2% below entry) ignore market structure entirely — they will stop you out of good trades in volatile names and leave you exposed in quiet ones. Let the chart, not your comfort, set the distance.

The ATR method for volatility-adjusted stops

Average True Range (ATR) measures a security's typical movement. Placing stops at 1.5–2x ATR from entry adapts automatically: volatile stocks get wider stops, calm ones tighter — each calibrated to survive normal noise while catching genuine reversals.

Combine both methods: find the structural invalidation point, check it against 1.5–2x ATR, and if they wildly disagree, reconsider the trade. Agreement between structure and volatility is a green flag.

Five stop-loss mistakes to eliminate

One: no stop at all — 'mental stops' fail the moment emotions spike. Two: moving stops further when price approaches (the stop was your plan; moving it is abandoning the plan). Three: stops so tight that normal noise triggers them — death by a thousand small cuts. Four: identical stops for different volatilities. Five: removing stops 'just until it comes back' — the sentence that precedes every blown account. Fix these five and your equity curve transforms.

Stop-loss order types in Indian markets: SL vs SL-M explained

Indian brokers offer two stop-loss order types and confusing them is costly. A stop-loss limit order (SL) triggers at your price but executes as a limit order — in a fast crash, the market can blow through your limit leaving the order unfilled while losses mount. A stop-loss market order (SL-M) triggers and executes immediately at the best available price — guaranteed fill, but with slippage in violent moves. For liquid large-caps in normal conditions, SL with a small limit buffer works well; around news events or in illiquid stocks, SL-M's guaranteed exit is usually worth the slippage cost. Know which you are placing before volatility decides for you.

Also master GTT (Good Till Triggered) orders where your broker offers them: stop-loss and target orders that persist across sessions, essential for swing traders who cannot watch screens all day. And understand the broker's auto-square-off policies — intraday positions get squared off at defined times (often 3:15–3:20 PM) regardless of your plans, so never assume a position survives past the cutoff. The mechanics of your exits deserve as much study as your entries: a perfect stop-loss level is useless if the order type fails you in the moment it matters.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I place my stop-loss?

At the price where your trade thesis is invalidated — below support/structure for longs, above for shorts — sanity-checked against 1.5–2x ATR so normal volatility doesn't trigger it.

Should I use a fixed percentage stop-loss?

Fixed percentages ignore market structure and volatility. They are better than no stop, but structure-plus-ATR placement is significantly more effective.

What is a trailing stop?

A stop that moves favourably with price, locking in profits while giving the trade room. Use structure-based trails (below rising swing lows) rather than arbitrary distances for best results.
