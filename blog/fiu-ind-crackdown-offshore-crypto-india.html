FIU-IND's September Crackdown: 15 Offshore Crypto Platforms Named — What It Means for Indian Traders

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 30, 2026·6 min read

← Back to all articles

On 9 September 2026, India's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND) issued non-compliance notices to 15 offshore crypto platforms for operating in the country without following India's anti-money laundering rules — and asked that their apps and websites be removed from public access in India. It is one of the largest enforcement actions against offshore crypto venues since the notices to Binance, Kraken and KuCoin in late 2023, and it lands when crypto has never been more popular in India: Chainalysis ranks the country number one in global crypto adoption, and CoinSwitch's co-founder Ashish Singhal puts the number of Indian crypto investors at 70 to 90 million.

The timing is no accident.

The 15 platforms named

The notices were issued under Section 13 of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002. The platforms named: Weex, Blofin, Rezorex, Bitunix, DigiFinex, Toobit, XT.com, Latoken, WOO X, Pionex, ChangeNOW, SimpleSwap, Fixedfloat, WhiteBIT and Guardarian — all described as small and medium-sized operators.

Alongside the notices, FIU-IND directed takedowns of the companies' applications and web URLs — making app and website availability the immediate enforcement focus. The government has not said when each app or URL would disappear, so traders on these platforms should expect disruption, not negotiation.

Why FIU is acting now

This did not come out of nowhere. Since March 2023, virtual digital asset service providers have been treated as reporting entities under India's anti-money laundering framework. Any crypto platform serving Indian users — regardless of whether it has an office in India — must register with FIU-IND, verify its customers, maintain records and report suspicious activity. At last count in 2026, around 50 crypto exchanges were registered with FIU-IND.

The enforcement drive follows the traders, not the other way around. The 1% TDS on crypto transfers, introduced in the Union Budget 2022-23, pushed spot trading out of the country: WazirX founder Nischal Shetty says 90–95% of spot volume evaporated from Indian exchanges after the tax announcement, and KoinX data for FY25 puts nearly 73% of Indian crypto trading volume on offshore platforms.

Then there is the workaround economy. The Economic Times reported that Indian users have been moving stablecoins like USDT to platforms in Sweden, Germany and Singapore, which convert the crypto into gift cards used in India for groceries, fuel and gold — keeping the money off Indian exchanges and off the authorities' radar. FIU-IND used the announcement to caution the public that "crypto products and NFTs are unregulated and can be highly risky" and that "there may be no regulatory recourse for any loss from such transactions."

What has not changed

It is worth being precise about the legal position, because confusion is the scammer's favourite tool:

  • Crypto is not banned in India. Virtual digital assets are neither legal tender nor prohibited. The Supreme Court struck down the RBI's April 2018 banking circular in March 2020, which allowed banks to process crypto transactions again.
  • The tax regime is unchanged. Gains on transfer are taxed at a flat 30% plus applicable cess, with a 1% TDS on specified transfers — and losses cannot be set off against other income or carried forward. The Union Budget 2026 did not amend these provisions. There is also an 18% GST on services provided by crypto exchanges.
  • The regulator's own chief is candid. A parliamentary panel chief put it plainly in September: "The government is not accepting VDAs, not regulating either." The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance has noted that excluding these assets from the definition of securities creates a regulatory grey area.
  • The RBI remains opposed. The central bank has made it clear it sees virtual assets as a financial-stability risk, while pushing its own alternative — the Digital Rupee (CBDC) pilots.

The CBI's P2P warning

Two days after FIU-IND acted, the Central Bureau of Investigation issued its own advisory on 11 September, aimed squarely at peer-to-peer traders. The warning: an above-market offer for USDT may involve money obtained through fraud, and one careless trade can turn a crypto seller into a suspect.

The CBI's guidance is blunt — use FIU-registered exchanges, never arrange deals through Telegram or WhatsApp, reject third-party payments, and report cybercrime by calling 1930 or visiting cybercrime.gov.in. If you buy or sell crypto peer-to-peer with UPI payments, this advisory was written for you.

What this means for your trading

Six practical takeaways for Indian crypto traders this week:

  • If you use a named platform, expect disruption. Apps and URLs can vanish with little warning. Sort out your access and records now — and assume withdrawals and transfers could be interrupted.
  • Trade on FIU-registered exchanges. Registration with FIU-IND is the minimum legitimacy test. Check that your exchange is on the registered list before trusting it with your money.
  • Offshore no longer means invisible. In July 2026 the Central Board of Direct Taxes issued a guidance note on the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), which will apply from 2027. As one tax lawyer put it: the taxman will start seeing your offshore wallets and foreign trades too. Anyone who assumed moving to an international exchange kept them under the radar is, in his words, "in for a surprise."
  • P2P plus UPI is now a red zone. The CBI warning makes peer-to-peer deals materially riskier — not just from scammers, but from ending up on the wrong side of an investigation into someone else's fraud money.
  • Keep records anyway. The 30% tax and 1% TDS apply regardless of which platform or country your trades happened in. Enforcement on reporting is tightening too — exchanges face real penalties for reporting failures starting April 2026.
  • Do not count on recourse. The finance ministry's own line is that there "may be no regulatory recourse" for losses in crypto products. Treat unregulated venues accordingly.

What happens next

The bigger story is still unwritten. The Parliamentary Standing Committee on Finance is holding separate discussions on virtual digital assets and the way forward, with its report expected in the winter session of Parliament — and sources suggest it may recommend strict curbs. In the interim, the committee has floated the idea of recognised self-regulatory organisations operating under a designated regulator.

The industry, meanwhile, is pushing self-regulation led by the Bharat Web3 Association. WazirX's Nischal Shetty says there is "some progress in regulatory clarity in terms of taxation, KYC and AML through FIU registration" — but admits it is "slower than in other countries."

The backdrop is hard to argue with: the OECD's Asia Capital Markets Report 2026 warns that growing interconnectedness between crypto and traditional markets creates contagion risk, alongside illicit-finance, cybersecurity and consumer-protection concerns. And traders keep trading anyway — Bitcoin crossed $80,000 for the first time since May 2026 and touched $81,235 on 25 August. The gap between India's crypto adoption and its crypto regulation has never been wider, and September's crackdown is the government trying to close it.

Frequently asked questions

Is crypto banned in India?

No. Crypto is neither legal tender nor prohibited in India. The Supreme Court struck down the RBI's banking ban in March 2020. Gains are taxed at a flat 30% plus cess with a 1% TDS on transfers — but virtual digital assets remain unregulated, and the finance ministry warns there may be no regulatory recourse for any loss.

I trade on one of the 15 named platforms. What should I do?

FIU-IND has sought takedown of the named platforms' apps and URLs in India, so expect service disruptions. Move your activity to FIU-IND-registered exchanges, keep complete records of your trades, and remember the 30% tax and 1% TDS apply regardless of which platform you used.

Will my offshore crypto trades be visible to the tax department?

In July 2026 the Central Board of Direct Taxes issued a guidance note on the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), which will apply from 2027. Tax lawyers say this means Indian authorities will start seeing offshore wallets and foreign trades as well — so trading abroad no longer keeps activity off the radar.
