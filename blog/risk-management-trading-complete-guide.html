Risk Management in Trading: The Complete Guide (2026)

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Ask professionals what separates survivors from casualties and the answer is never 'better entries'. It is risk management — the system of rules governing how much you can lose, when you stop, and how positions combine. Strategy is the engine; risk management is the brakes, steering and seatbelt.

This is the complete framework, from per-trade rules to portfolio-level thinking.

Think in R-multiples, not rupees

Professionals measure trades in R — units of initial risk. A trade risking ₹5,000 to make ₹10,000 is a 2R opportunity; one risking ₹5,000 to make ₹2,500 is 0.5R. Thinking in R detaches decisions from money emotions and makes strategies comparable: a 40%-win-rate system at 2R is profitable, while a 60% system at 0.5R is not.

Journal every trade in R-multiples (see our journal guide). Your average R per trade is the truest single measure of your trading skill.

Portfolio heat and correlation

Five positions each risking 1% is not 1% total risk if they are correlated — five long Nifty-bank-adjacent stocks are effectively one big bet. 'Portfolio heat' — total risk across open positions — should stay under 5–6% for most traders, with correlated positions counted together.

Check correlation before adding positions: same sector, same theme or same macro driver means shared risk. Diversification across genuinely different drivers is what actually reduces heat.

Drawdown rules: the circuit breakers

Define drawdown circuit breakers in advance: at −6% monthly, halve position sizes; at −10%, stop trading for the week and review; at −20% overall, stop entirely and rebuild on paper. These rules feel unnecessary until the month you need them — which is precisely when discretion fails.

Write them down, sign them, give a copy to someone who will hold you accountable. Risk rules only work when breaking them has consequences.

The risk policy document: writing your personal constitution

Professional trading firms govern risk with written policy documents — and individual traders should too. Your personal risk constitution is a one-page document specifying: maximum risk per trade (1%), maximum portfolio heat (5–6%), maximum daily loss (−2 to −3%, then done for the day), maximum monthly drawdown (−8 to −10%, then halve size and review), and the conditions under which you stop trading entirely and return to paper. It also defines correlation rules (no more than X% of heat in one sector), news-event rules (reduce size into binary events), and the review cadence (weekly journal review, monthly strategy audit). Write it, print it, sign it.

The document's power is pre-commitment: decisions made in calm rationality constrain behaviour in emotional storms. Review it every quarter and amend only in calm periods — never mid-drawdown, when the temptation is to loosen rules rather than tighten them. Share it with a trading buddy or mentor who can ask uncomfortable questions when you deviate. Most traders know every risk rule in this guide intellectually; the ones who survive have converted knowledge into documented, witnessed commitment. Your risk policy is the difference between understanding risk management and practising it.

Frequently asked questions

What is portfolio heat in trading?

The total risk across all open positions, expressed as a percentage of capital. Keeping heat under 5–6% ensures no single market event can inflict catastrophic damage, even if every position fails simultaneously.

How do I handle correlated positions?

Treat correlated positions (same sector, theme or macro driver) as partially one position for risk purposes. Either reduce size on each or diversify across genuinely uncorrelated drivers.

What should I do after hitting my max drawdown?

Stop trading real capital immediately, switch to paper trading, and conduct a structured review of what broke — strategy, discipline or market regime. Resume only with a written diagnosis and fix.
