SEBI's Project Jagrook: New Awareness Warnings on Broker Apps and Websites — What Traders Must Know

Trading Expo India editorial team·October 3, 2026·6 min read

← Back to all articles

Open your broker's app or website this month and you are likely to see something new before you reach the charts: a regulator-written message reminding you to invest with caution. On 1 October 2026, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) directed all stock brokers to prominently display standardised investor awareness messages on their websites and mobile trading applications — the latest move under Project Jagrook, its nationwide investor education initiative.

The timing is deliberate. The rollout begins on 5 October, the same day the tenth edition of IOSCO's World Investor Week starts — a global campaign that this year is themed around investor resilience, digital deception and scam alert. SEBI's message to brokers, in effect: the education material can no longer sit in a corner of your site.

What changes on broker websites

For broker websites the rollout has two stages, and both are mandatory:

  • 5 to 31 October 2026: brokers must display SEBI's investor awareness messages alongside the existing risk disclosures on their websites.
  • From 1 November 2026: the awareness messages must appear directly on the landing page of the broker's website — the first screen a visitor sees.

This builds on SEBI's May 2023 circular, which required brokers to display specified risk disclosures to clients. The regulator noted that the awareness creatives shared with intermediaries may be modified from time to time, and that all other provisions governing risk disclosures remain unchanged.

What changes on trading apps

For mobile trading apps — where most Indian retail trading now happens — SEBI has chosen a gentler on-ramp:

  • 5 to 31 October 2026: displaying the awareness messages on trading apps is voluntary. But there is a catch: if a broker chooses to show the awareness creatives on any given day, displaying the risk disclosures becomes optional for that day.
  • From 1 November 2026: display becomes mandatory. Brokers must show the investor awareness messages and the risk disclosures on alternate days, on the landing page of the trading app.
From 1 November: awareness messages mandatory on broker website landing pages — and on trading apps, awareness messages and risk disclosures on alternate days.

The alternate-day design is an honest piece of regulatory psychology. A permanent banner becomes wallpaper; rotating messages have a better chance of actually being read. Whether it works will depend on what the creatives say — SEBI has shared the awareness messages with intermediaries but has not published the exact wording for the public to judge.

Exchanges and depositories are in the net too

The circular does not stop with brokers. Stock exchanges and depositories have been directed to communicate the requirements to their members and participants — and to display the investor awareness messages on their own websites as well. When NSE and BSE themselves carry the banners, the campaign gets hard to miss.

Why SEBI is pushing this now

The context is the week it coincides with. World Investor Week 2026, run by the International Organization of Securities Commissions (IOSCO), takes place from 5 to 11 October 2026 across more than 100 jurisdictions. The tenth edition's primary themes are Investor Resilience, Digital Deception and Scam Alert, under the tagline "Invest in Your Future. Protect It Today." — a direct response to increasingly sophisticated online fraud and misleading investment content on social media.

India has its own reasons for the urgency. SEBI's August 2026 study of equity derivatives found that 87.7% of individual F&O traders ended FY26 in net loss, with aggregate retail losses of ₹91,685 crore — a figure that makes investor education less of a formality and more of a necessity. When nine out of ten retail F&O traders lose money, the regulator's instinct to put warnings where traders actually look is understandable.

There is also the scam angle. Project Jagrook's emphasis on digital deception lands at a time when fake "SEBI-registered" advisors, clone trading apps and Telegram tip groups are a standing problem for Indian retail investors. A standardised awareness banner on every legitimate broker's app at least gives traders a reference point for what genuine investor communication looks like.

What traders should actually do with these warnings

A banner is only useful if it changes behaviour. Five practical takeaways:

  • Read them, don't dismiss them. The creatives may rotate and be updated periodically. If SEBI changes the message, it is because the threat landscape changed.
  • A banner is not a trust badge. Every compliant broker must display these messages — they say nothing about the quality of the broker itself. Continue to verify a broker's SEBI registration number and exchange memberships before opening an account.
  • Watch for the scams the campaign warns about. The World Investor Week theme of digital deception is the one that matters most: be sceptical of guaranteed returns, "SEBI-certified" tip providers and anyone asking you to move money off a regulated platform.
  • Respect the risk disclosures too. From November, apps show awareness messages and risk disclosures on alternate days — both exist because the statistics behind them are grim. Read our breakdown of SEBI's F&O study to see what the risk disclosures are summarising.
  • Use the campaign week. World Investor Week runs 5–11 October, and brokers, exchanges and depositories will be running education activities all week. It is a good seven days to audit your own habits — leverage use, position sizing, broker fees.

What happens next

The phased deadlines are the near-term story: 5 October for websites, 1 November for the full mandate across websites and apps. Beyond that, two things are worth watching. First, whether SEBI publishes the actual awareness creatives — the wording will determine whether this reads as genuine education or compliance wallpaper. Second, whether the campaign expands to other intermediaries: mutual fund distributors, investment advisers and research analysts are not in this circular's scope, but Project Jagrook is described as a nationwide initiative, and brokers are rarely where such campaigns end.

For traders, the message is simple enough to fit on a banner: the regulator is spending real effort to get your attention before you place the trade. Give it the few seconds it asks for.

Frequently asked questions

What is SEBI's Project Jagrook circular of October 2026?

On 1 October 2026 SEBI directed all stock brokers to prominently display standardised investor awareness messages on their websites and mobile trading apps under Project Jagrook, its nationwide investor education initiative. Websites must show the messages from 5 October, with mandatory display on the landing page from 1 November; on trading apps the display is voluntary until 31 October, then mandatory on alternate days from 1 November.

What is World Investor Week 2026?

World Investor Week is IOSCO's global campaign for investor education and protection. The tenth edition runs from 5 to 11 October 2026 across more than 100 jurisdictions, with the primary themes Investor Resilience, Digital Deception and Scam Alert, and the tagline "Invest in Your Future. Protect It Today." SEBI's Project Jagrook rollout coincides with the campaign.

Should I trust a broker's website just because it shows SEBI's awareness messages?

No. The messages are a regulator-supplied education banner that every compliant broker must show — they are not a trust badge for the broker itself. Always verify a broker's SEBI registration number, check its exchange memberships, and watch for red flags such as guaranteed returns or pressure to deposit. The awareness messages are there to remind you to do exactly that.
