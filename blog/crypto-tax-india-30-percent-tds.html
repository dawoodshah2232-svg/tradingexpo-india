Crypto Tax in India: The 30% Rule and 1% TDS Explained (2026)

Trading Expo India editorial team·September 24, 2026·3 min read

← Back to all articles

Since 2022, India has taxed virtual digital assets (VDAs) — including cryptocurrencies and NFTs — under one of the strictest regimes anywhere: a flat 30% tax on gains plus 1% TDS on transfers. Four years on, the framework still surprises traders who assumed crypto profits were somehow invisible to the tax department.

This is an explainer, not tax advice — but every Indian crypto trader needs to understand these mechanics before the next profitable trade.

The 30% flat tax: how it works

Gains from transferring VDAs are taxed at a flat 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess), regardless of whether you held for a day or a year — there is no long-term/short-term distinction as with equities. Only the cost of acquisition can be deducted; no other expenses, and crucially, losses from crypto cannot be set off against any other income or carried forward.

That last point is the regime's sharpest edge: a trader who makes ₹10 lakh on one coin and loses ₹9 lakh on another still pays 30% on the ₹10 lakh gain. The losses simply vanish for tax purposes.

The 1% TDS on every transfer

Buyers must deduct 1% TDS on VDA transfers above specified thresholds, with the deducted amount deposited against the seller's PAN. On compliant Indian exchanges this is typically handled automatically — which is convenient, but also means your trading activity is fully visible to the tax authorities.

Peer-to-peer and cross-exchange traders must handle TDS themselves, and mistakes here compound quickly across hundreds of trades. High-frequency traders should use portfolio-tracking software that computes TDS obligations trade by trade.

Record-keeping is your only defence

Maintain a complete ledger: every buy, sell, swap, transfer and fee, with dates, quantities, rupee values and counterparty details. Reconstructing a year's DeFi activity from memory during an assessment is a nightmare you can avoid with thirty minutes of weekly bookkeeping.

Consider a qualified chartered accountant familiar with VDA taxation before filing season, not during it. The cost of advice is trivial next to the cost of a misfiled return in a regime this strict.

Advance tax, penalties and the compliance calendar

The 30% tax is only half the obligation; the other half is timing. If your crypto gains are significant, advance tax instalments apply through the financial year — waiting until filing season to pay the full liability can attract interest under the advance tax provisions. Each quarter, estimate your realised VDA gains, compute the liability, and pay the instalment due. This quarterly rhythm also forces the bookkeeping habit that makes filing season painless: traders who reconcile every three months never face the nightmare of reconstructing a year's trades in March.

Penalties escalate with negligence: under-reporting or misreporting income can attract penalties on top of the tax and interest, and the data-matching infrastructure — exchange-reported TDS, AIS statements, bank records — makes discrepancies increasingly visible to the department. The compliance calendar worth maintaining: quarterly advance tax dates, TDS reconciliation against Form 26AS after each quarter, and a pre-filing review with your CA a month before the due date. Boring, systematic compliance is the rational strategy in a regime this strict — the expected cost of cutting corners exceeds any short-term saving.

Frequently asked questions

What is the crypto tax rate in India?

Gains from virtual digital assets are taxed at a flat 30% plus surcharge and cess. Only the cost of acquisition is deductible, and crypto losses cannot be set off against other income or carried forward. (Framework as of 2026 — confirm current provisions with a tax professional.)

What is the 1% TDS on crypto in India?

A 1% tax deducted at source applies on VDA transfers above thresholds, deposited against the seller's PAN. Compliant Indian exchanges usually handle it automatically; P2P traders must manage it themselves.

Can I offset crypto losses against crypto gains?

No. Under the current framework, losses from VDAs cannot be set off against gains from VDAs or any other income, and cannot be carried forward to future years.

Do I need to report crypto even without withdrawing to INR?

Crypto-to-crypto swaps are transfers that can trigger tax events. Maintain records of all swaps, not just INR withdrawals, and take professional advice on your reporting obligations.
